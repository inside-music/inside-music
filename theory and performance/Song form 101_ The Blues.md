## *Song Form 101: The 12-Bar Blues*

## ***History of the blues***

* Born in slavery on the Mississippi delta,   
* Blues emerged from African American field hollers, work songs, spirituals   
* Mixed with Afro-Caribbean and European music in New Orleans to give rise to jazz, Zydeco and Cajun  
* Music moved along with freed people in the Great Migration up the Mississippi through Memphis and on to Chicago and all points between. It mixed with European-American big band and mountain music giving rise to country, rhythm and blues, an rock and roll  
* 12-bar form is most common blues song structure  
* Swing rhythm (12/8 shuffle) with “push beats” 

## ***Harmonic structure: I-IV-V, but different***

* Starts with the I-IV-V of European classical diatonic harmony, but breaks a lot of rules  
  * The I and IV chords are usually  played as seventh (dominant) chords  
  * “blue notes”: minor third, flat fifth, and flat (dominant) seventh. The “blues scale” is a pentatonic minor scale with the addition of a flat five. 

## ***The 12-bar song form***

* Three lines, each with four bars  
  * First line centers on the 1 chord  
  * Second line centers on the 4  
  * Third line centers on the 5, with a “turnaround” (cadence) leading harmonically back to the 1  
* There are many variations, but a typical 12-bar blues looks like this, with all chords played as sevenths:


  |1    |4    |1    |1    |  
  |4    |4    |1    |1    |  
  |5    |4    |1    |5    | 

    
* For the key of E, it looks like this, again with all chords played as sevenths:


  |E    |A    |E    |E    |  
  |A    |A    |E    |E    |  
  |B    |A    |E    |B    |
