## ***Live sound Basics***

#### Components of the PA system

* Source inputs  
  * Microphones
    * Converts sound pressure waves into a small electric signal  
    * Signal is (usually) low impedance and balanced  
    * uses an _XLR_ cable
    * plugs directly into a mixer channel
  * Instruments (electric guitar/bass, keyboards, etc)  
    * Signal is high impedance and unbalanced  
    * Usually ¼ inch _TS phone plug_
    * Often needs a _Direct Input_ (_DI_) transformer to plug into a mic-level mixer or powered speaker, but can sometimes plug directly into a mixer or powered speaker  
* Mixer  
  * Multiple inputs, each with _channel strip_
    * Input level or _gain_ \- adjusts the level of pre-amplification used to bring the electrical signal from mic or instrument level to line level   
    * _Equalization_ (_EQ_)  
    * auxiliary bus sends (_aux_ or _mon_) for effects or monitors
    * Level _fader_ controls output of channel to 
  * Main section with output controls and meters  
    * Independent control of main, monitor and auxiliary outputs  
 * Powered Speakers  
    * Use a balanced, line level output from a mixer.   
      * usually XLR “mic” cable, but can also be a stereo phone, or TRS (tip-ring-sleeve ¼ inch phone plug), or a combo TRS/XLR cable  
      * Can also run unbalanced line-level with TS instrument cables, but this is not suitable over any distance   
    * Often have a built in mic-level preamp so you can just plug a mic in, some have instrument inputs
 
### Operation
* Plug in speaker power cord
* Plug in mixer power cord
* Connect components
 * each microphone and instrument to its own channel strip on the mixer
 * mixer to speakers: main outs L/R to speaker inputs. Make sure speaker inputs are for line-level, not mic. Often there is a switch.
 * Startup
  * Turn on mixer with master outputs all the way down (-∞)
  * Turn on speakers, set output do 0 dBu
  * Normalize mixer inputs; EQ at mid points, gain and output levels all the way down (-∞)
    * **Important:** never plug or unplug a source without turning down or muting the channel!
  * raise master faders to -3
  * For each channel 
   * with somebody producing sound, gradually increase the gain until the clip or overload light flashes, then reduce until it no longer flashes.  
     * Raise level fader to what sounds like a good level.
     * If you have to raise it above -12, make sure gain is high enough
     * If you get no sound, check if there is a mute button on the channel and the main
     * Adjust EQ as needed to clarify sound and reduce feedback
  * Adjust master faders to suit the performance. Between -3 and 0 dBu normal

* Vocabulary / glossary
  * Amplifier: raises the line level enough to drive speakers
  * Balanced/unbalanced
   * Balanced signals travel on 3-conductor cables, eliminate noise
   * Unblanced signals travel on 2-conductor cables, susceptible to noise
  * Cables
   * _XLR_, _Cannon_ or _mic_ cable: A shielded, 3-conductor cable with latching 3-pin connectors, male on one end and female on the other. Commanly called a "mic cable", but also used for balanced, line-level signals (e.g. from board to amp or powered speakers)
   * _TS_ or _TRS Phone_ cable: A shielded, unbalanced cable with a ¼ inch male _phone plug_ on both ends. _TS_ or "tip-sleeve" cables are used for musical instruments. _TRS_ or "tip-ring-sleeve" cables are used for balanced, line-level signals.
  * Channel strip: a section of mixer used to condition a single input soure
  * Direct Box or DI: a transformer that provides a high-impedance input with a low impedance output. Typically used to plug a guitar into a mixer.
  * Equalizer or EQ: section of a channel strip or guitar amp that adjusts the relative levels of different frequencies, often high, mid and low, sometimes _parametric_ or _graphic_.
  * Impedance: the resistance of a device, measured across its outputs. Mics are typically low impedance, guitars typically high impedance. Source impedance must be equal to or less than the impedance of the mixer or interface it plugs into.
  * Level: the signal strength measured in decibels 
   * mic: -60 to -40 dBu, or 1 to 10 mV
   * instrument: -30 to -20 du, or 20 to 100 or more mV
   * line: -10 dBu (consumer) or -4 dBu (pro standard), 1 V nominal

  * Pre-amplifier: raises the input signal level of a mic or instrument to line level
