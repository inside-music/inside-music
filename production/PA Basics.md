## ***Live sound Basics***

#### **The signal chain**

* Source inputs  
  * Microphones  
    * Converts sound pressure waves into a small electric signal  
    * Signal is (usually) low impedance and balanced  
    * cable usually has XLR latching 3-pin connectors on both ends  
    * plugs directly into a mixer  
  * Instruments (electric guitar/bass, keyboards, etc)  
    * Signal is high impedance and unbalanced  
    * Usually ¼ inch phone plug (*Tip-sleeve, or TS)*  
    * Often needs a “Direct Input” (DI) transformer to plug into a mic-level mixer or powered speaker, but can sometimes plug directly into a mixer or powered speaker  
* Mixer  
  * Multiple inputs  
    * Input level or gain \- adjusts the level of pre-amplification used to bring the electrical signal from mic level to line level   
    * Equalization (EQ)  
    * Monitor and auxiliary send busses  
    * Main fader  
  * Main section with output controls and meters  
    * Independent control of main, monitor and auxiliary outputs  
  * Startup  
    * Normalize inputs; EQ at mid points, gain and output levels 0  
      * **Important:** never plug or unplug a source without zeroing all outputs for the channel to avoid speaker damage  
    * For each input,   
      * Plug in source  
      * gradually increase the gain until the clip or overload light flashes, then reduce until it no longer flashes.  
      * Raise master fader to about \-12, then raise channel fader to proper level
* Powered Speakers  
    * Use a balanced, line level output from a mixer.   
      * usually XLR “mic” cable, but can also be a stereo phone, or TRS (tip-ring-sleeve ¼ inch phone plug), or a combo TRS/XLR cable  
      * Can also run unbalanced line-level with TS instrument cables, but this is not suitable over any distance   
    * Often have a built in mic-level preamp so you can just plug a mic in, some have instrument inputs
